## Overview

The AI Context Framework is a documentation and governance framework. It structures how projects describe themselves to AI assistants. It is **not** an AI system, model, or decision-making engine, and it does not execute AI inference.

This page documents the responsible AI practices that apply to the framework itself and the guidance the framework provides to adopters.

## AI-Assisted Content Disclosure

This framework was developed with assistance from GitHub Copilot. Content is reviewed by human
maintainers, but may contain errors or become outdated. Validate guidance against your
organization's requirements, current authoritative documentation, security and compliance
policies, and applicable laws before implementation.

This project does not constitute official Microsoft, government, legal, security, or compliance
guidance. Use is at your own risk.

## AI Output Is a Draft

**AI-generated output — including output produced with the help of this framework's context files — is a draft that requires human review before use in production, publication, or any consequential decision.**

This includes:
- Code generated by GitHub Copilot or any other AI assistant
- Documentation, context files, or architecture decisions drafted by AI
- Any content produced by an AI assistant that has read `.ai/context.md` or other framework files

The framework structures context to improve AI output quality. It does not guarantee correctness, completeness, or compliance. Human engineers remain accountable for all decisions and artifacts.

---

## Model Limitations

AI assistants — including GitHub Copilot — are probabilistic models. Even when given clear, well-structured instructions:

- They may not follow all instructions in every response
- They may produce plausible-sounding but incorrect or outdated information
- They may misinterpret context or miss important constraints
- Their behavior may differ across model versions, prompts, and sessions

The framework is designed to improve the likelihood that Copilot reads and applies your `.ai/context.md`. It cannot guarantee this behavior. Use "Copilot is instructed to …" — not "Copilot will …" — when describing expected AI behavior to your team.

## Human Oversight and Accountability

The framework's governance model preserves human oversight at multiple points:

| Control point | Human role |
|---------------|-----------|
| PR review | Every change must be reviewed and approved by a human maintainer |
| Registry entries | Adoption records require human sign-off |
| Product ADRs | Architecture decisions are human-authored and human-approved |
| `.ai/context.md` | Owned by a named human role; reviewed on a defined cadence |
| CI validation | Automated checks surface staleness and drift; humans act on alerts |

No AI agent in this framework has merge authority or unilateral commit rights.

## Privacy and PII

**Never include the following in `.ai/` context files, templates, or any file committed to a repository:**

- Credentials, API keys, passwords, or secrets
- Connection strings or environment-specific configuration
- Personally Identifiable Information (PII) of individuals
- Personal commentary about teammates

The framework's `org/standards.md` and `.gitignore` enforce this at the standards and tooling levels. The Tier-3 `.ai_local/` directory (personal, gitignored) is the correct place for session-specific personal context.

Adopters are responsible for ensuring their own `.ai/` directories comply with their organization's data handling and privacy requirements.

## RAG and Retrieval Considerations

When this framework's outputs are used as a knowledge source for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines or vector search systems:

- **Data retention:** Adopters are responsible for understanding what their RAG system retains and for how long. Ingested `.ai/` content may persist in indexes beyond the lifetime of the source document.
- **Staleness in retrieval:** A retrieved context chunk may be outdated even if the source file has been updated. Framework staleness tooling helps detect drift in the source files, but does not govern downstream index freshness.
- **PII and sensitive data:** If `.ai/` content is ingested into a shared or organization-wide RAG index, the same PII/secrets prohibition applies. Ensure content is reviewed before ingestion.
- **Model grounding:** RAG retrieval improves AI grounding but does not eliminate model hallucination. Human review remains required.

## Fairness and Representational Guidance

AI context files describe systems, teams, and decisions. Adopters should be aware of the following:

- **Avoid demographic assumptions** in context files. Do not describe users, customers, or stakeholders in ways that embed unstated demographic or cultural assumptions.
- **Use inclusive language** when describing roles, teams, and workflows. Context files are read by AI and by humans; language norms apply equally.
- **Naming conventions and domain terms** in context files influence AI suggestions. Review them for unintentional bias or exclusion before committing.
- **AI-generated content** (including Copilot suggestions produced with this framework's context) may reflect biases present in the model's training data. Human review is the control point.

The framework does not audit your context files for fairness; that responsibility belongs to your team.

## No Compliance Guarantee

This framework does not certify, guarantee, or imply compliance with any regulatory framework, privacy regulation, AI governance standard, or security certification, including but not limited to:

- GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, or any other privacy regulation
- SOC 2, ISO 27001, or any security certification
- Microsoft Responsible AI Standard or any AI governance framework
- Any named AI Act, executive order, or regulatory requirement

Adopters are responsible for assessing whether the framework's use in their context is consistent with their compliance obligations.
